  1. Air. The gas turbine's compressor pulls about 1.2 tonnes of air a second in through the filter house and squeezes it to about 23 times atmospheric pressure.
  2. Gas turbine. Gas burns in the ring of combustors at about 1,480 °C, and the hot gas spins the turbine, which turns the compressor and the generator.
  3. Heat recovery. The exhaust leaves at over 600 °C and flows through the HRSG's tube banks, hottest first, boiling water at three pressures.
  4. Steam turbine. The steam drives a second turbine on the same shaft, then condenses under vacuum, cooled by the fans of the cooling cells.
  5. Generator. One generator at 3,000 rpm; the transformer steps its output up for the grid. Two thirds comes from gas, a third from steam, from the same fuel.
  6. Carbon capture. The flue gas is cooled, then rises through the absorber where an amine solvent takes up about 95 % of its CO₂. In the stripper, steam from the turbine drives it off again; it is compressed and piped away to be stored under the sea.
